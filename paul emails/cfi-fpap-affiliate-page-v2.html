Q: Is CFI FPAP worth it if I already have an MBA or CPA?

A: Yes, especially if your MBA or CPA didn't include deep FP&A training. Most MBA programs cover finance broadly (corporate finance, investments, valuation) but don't go deep on budgeting, forecasting, and FP&A processes.

Similarly, CPAs are experts in accounting and tax, but FP&A is forward-looking analysis—a different skill set. I've worked with many CPAs who took FPAP to transition into FP&A roles.

Q: What's the difference between FPAP and FPAC?

A: FPAP (CFI) and FPAC (AFP) are different certifications:

  • FPAP: Self-paced online courses ($298-$508), comprehensive curriculum, no exam proctoring required
  • FPAC: Credentialed exam-based certification ($305-$1,000+), requires proctored exam, more rigorous but also more expensive

I recommend FPAP for most people because it's more comprehensive and affordable. FPAC is better if you specifically want a credentialed certification (like CPA or CFA style).

Q: How is FPAP different from Wall Street Prep or other financial modeling courses?

A: Wall Street Prep focuses on investment banking modeling (LBOs, M&A, DCF valuation). FPAP focuses on corporate FP&A (budgeting, forecasting, variance analysis, business partnering).

If you want to work in investment banking, private equity, or corporate development, Wall Street Prep is better. If you want to work in FP&A at operating companies, FPAP is better.

Many people do both! FPAP first for corporate finance fundamentals, then Wall Street Prep if they want to specialize in M&A or valuation.
