Stay out of Excel Hell

​

Microsoft Excel Tables

Microsoft Excel Tables continue to be emphasized more and more by Microsoft due to the many advantages they have over data ranges in Excel. I believe they are something every Finance professional should learn. I am such a big fan of tables that when I was asked to provide the Excel content for the Wharton Online and Wall Street Prep FP&A Certificate I included an entire section on Excel Tables.

​

Question: What are Excel Tables?

​

Answer: A Microsoft Excel table is a structured range that facilitates data management by organizing information in a tabular format. It allows for efficient sorting, filtering, and analysis, enhancing data accessibility and manipulation.

​

You are probably wondering why Excel Tables are so important. Excel tables are basically the entry point to Modern Excel. In order to use the following tools in Microsoft Excel you have to learn about using tables.

​

➡️ Microsoft Excel CoPilot - Copilot requires your data to be in tables in order to analyze your data.

​

➡️ Power Query - Power Query uses tables for managing your data

​

➡️ Power Pivot - Power Pivot places all your data in tables

​

If you want to improve your Excel skills, learning Microsoft Excel tables is a must. As George Mount, Excel MVP and Analytics expert put it Excel Tables are the gateway to Modern Excel. Business Partner's Corner

Great business partnership includes taking care of yourself first!

Finance and Accounting folks often neglect themselves!

I remember many late nights finalizing a budget or month-end. With exhaustion comes bad decisions.

​

  • We usually ordered the unhealthiest thing on the menu.
  • I wouldn’t exercise much during those periods.
  • I would consume more caffeine than usual.

​

All of this led to poor sleep and a vicious cycle (as my mind was usually racing trying to figure out how to reconcile the forecast or close the profit gap or a million other potential issues).

​

As I reflect on those crazy times and periods, I would tell my younger self to change this approach and take care of myself.

Here are a few things I would do:

​

1) Plan your week and plan your meals and snacks. Take fruits/veggies and healthy snacks and get your workout in early in the day.

​

2) If you can’t figure something out, ask for help. We often try to take on too much on our own. Ask your team, ask your peers, ask your manager, ask your cross-functional team. Just ASK!

​

3) Sleep on it. Many times, I would have an issue and would struggle to figure it out late at night which led to more frustration. Later in my Finance career, I would sleep on it and would figure it out the next day without much effort. The subconscious is always working on unfinished problems.

​

4) Have fun! My last few forecasts had their usual dose of pain, but they had very intentional fun built in.

​

5) Thank your team!

​

a. Schedule your end of forecast celebration. My team often went to a fantastic restaurant called ‘The Keg’ and celebrated with a drink and meal. We all looked forward to it.

​

b. At one of my last forecasts, which was especially painful (forecasting bathroom tissue during the pandemic), I asked our CEO (Dino Bianco) to join a team meeting with all the key players of the forecast. He sincerely thanked them for their hard work and dedication. They were stunned and appreciative.

​

Don’t sweat the small stuff! A forecast or budget is never perfect. Bad month-end results are not your fault, even if you are the messenger.

​

Keep calm, have fun, and take care of yourself.

The FP&A Guy's Tech Tips

​

Prompt Engineering

​

Many of you might be wondering what prompt engineering is and what it has to do with FP&A. Prompt engineering is the process of designing and optimizing prompts to effectively communicate with and guide language models.

​

With the recent explosion in LLM's, prompt engineering is more important than ever. If you have asked a question of Generative AI and got back a useless answer, there is a good chance you asked the question wrong. Learning to write your questions right will go along way to improving the quality and accuracy of the content AI produces.

​

When asking questions of Generative AI think about the following to improve the accuracy of the response you receive.

​

  • Clarity - Be as clear as possible in your prompts; do not use slang language and focus on being concise.
  • Context - Provide context and background details for the question being asked. Make sure to tell it the end goal and how the information will be used.
  • Specificity - Be very specific about the output desired, format, style, etc. If you want it to use emojis or a bullet list or output, it to a certain format type, make sure to state these things specifically.
  • Examples - When possible, provide an example of the desired output. If the tool allows it upload an example of what you are looking for.
  • Iteration - Remember Generative AI is designed to be conversational. If you do not get the answer the first time, keep refining your questions.

​

Prompt engineering is a skill that takes practice to get good at so if you do not get the answer, you want the first time, keep practicing.

Ron's Recommendations

​

It's never to late to be healthy, Kevin Brady

​

We often neglect ourselves and put ourselves last!

​

As I reflect on my Finance career, I neglected my physical and mental well being. I poured my heart and soul and all my energy into work (especially during budget and forecast season) and wish I focused more on my well being. My invitation to you is to learn from my mistakes and gradually build in habits that take care of your physical and mental health and put yourself first.

​

Kevin Brady shares his personal story of having a near death experience and how it reshaped his life. He talks about a moment in his life when his doctor was about to prescribe medication to him.

​

He took a stand and took charge of his health (nutrition and exercise) and within a few weeks his tests went from unhealthy to phenomenal. He didn’t need medication! He used healthy eating, mindfulness, exercise, and great sleeping habits.

​

Here are some of my key takeaways:

​

  • He talks about 4 key elements of health (eating well, exercise, mindfulness, and sleep) and his analogy is that they are wheels on a car. Keeping them in balance is important.

​

  • Start with small changes.
  • Follow the 80/20 rule. I love this rule of not having unrealistic targets. Give yourself the ‘cheat day’ and make it fun and enjoyable.
  • Failure is allowed. If you have a bad day, ‘don’t worry be happy’ and try again tomorrow.
  • Find activities that you like. Whether it is going to the gym or hiking or playing soccer, watching Excel videos while on the treadmill like the FP&A guy….do it your way and have fun!
  • Take initiative and advocate for your own health. Having regular health checks and blood work is important. Don’t put it off.

​

I have started making small changes and am already seeing benefits and feel better. I have a healthy shake every morning and have a 10-minute meditation built into my morning routine.

​

This morning, I walked to the local Coffee shop instead of taking an Uber from my garage. An awesome 20-minute walk which gave me the inspiration to write about health this week.

​

Kevin Brady is almost 60 years old. He looks about 40 years old and competes in triathlons around the world (while owning his own business). He definitely walks the walks!
