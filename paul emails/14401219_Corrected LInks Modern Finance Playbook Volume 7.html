Business Partner's Corner

Problem Solving and Decision Framework

​

As Finance professionals we constantly face problems or challenges. Having a consistent framework to solve these challenges is extremely helpful. Paul and I have developed a course for Finance departments to solve this challenge. We consulted experts in Finance and beyond on what approaches they use. Here is our six-pronged framework:

​

1) Define & align on the problem. Most experts agree that this is where we often fall short. We often jump to solutions before truly understanding the problem. One great technique to use is ‘the 5 whys’ which is often used in manufacturing settings to really understand the problem.

​

“It is critical to spend time on the problem. Step #1 is critical & often gets missed!” Vince Ambrosino (SVP Finance at McCain Foods)

​

“If you define the problem correctly, you almost have the solution.” Steve Jobs

​

2) Collect/gather information. This is pretty self-explanatory. The one area that often gets missed is gathering qualitative information by speaking to subject matter experts. This supplements the data and provides phenomenal context.

​

3) Analyze the data and input: Finance loves analyzing data. This could be a price/volume analysis on the P&L, an NPV or IRR calculation on a potential investment, or many other analysis methods.

​

4) Recommend: This is another massive area of opportunity for us in Finance. Instead of sitting on the fence, providing a recommendation and why we recommend it is a great business partnership approach.

​

5) Align / Sell your recommendation. Doing fantastic analysis and providing a recommendation leads naturally to developing conviction and an approach that confidently sells-in the recommendation. This requires a clear understanding of our stakeholders and what their natural questions and potential objections would be. Do not ignore this step as it is critical!

​

6) Execute – create execution timelines and checkpoints. Last but not least, business moves at lightning speed so creating timelines and checkpoints is critical to ensure the action gets implemented on time and on budget.

​

The magnitude of the problem dictates how much time you would devote to each step. A complex problem may benefit from going through all these steps in detail while a simple problem may not.

The FP&A Guy's Tech Tips

​

“75 percent of long-term job success depends on people skills, while only 25 percent on technical knowledge.”

​
― Peggy Klaus, The Hard Truth About Soft Skills: Soft Skills for Succeeding in a Hard World

​

The reality is improving your technical skills will only take you so far in your career. As I have often heard it said that technical skills will get you your first job and your soft skills will get you promoted. As finance professionals, I think it is important to have solid technical skills as a foundation, and this includes:

​

  • Excel
  • Financial Modeling
  • Data Analysis
  • SQL - at least the basics
  • Data Visualization/Business Intelligence

​

My view is that early in our careers, we all need to invest time in technical skills, but once we hit a level of proficiency and we can do that job well, learning the latest formula at the expense of focusing on soft skills does not pay. This does not mean we should not learn the newest formula or not stay current on technical skills it just means we should invest more time on soft skills.

​

If you want to be a leader, you must learn how to work with people, how to present the story, and how to influence the business. These skills will be invaluable as you move from individual contributor to manager. Make a commitment today to improve your soft skills, and your career will thank you.

Ron's Recommendations

​

The Miracle Morning by Hal Elrod

Finance is arguably the busiest function. Our to-do list seems to never end. Finding a daily and practical practice to develop a can-do attitude and glass half-full approach can be hard at times. This book was a game changer for me in terms of developing a winning morning routine that would help me ‘win the day’ more often than not as a Finance professional.

​

The beauty of this approach is that Hal brilliantly addresses the issue of us never having enough time for a morning routine. His challenge to all of us is that even if we have six minutes, that can make a positive difference.

​

Hal studied high performers and six habits that consistently showed up for these high performers. These six practices are designed to help individuals start their day with focus, energy, and positivity, leading to transformative changes over time. He created the SAVERS acronym to help us remember the habits (acronyms are awesome 😊). So even if you only dedicate 6 minutes per day for a consistent time period, doing one minute of each habit, it can make a significant difference.

​

  • S – Silence (closing your eyes for 1 minute can be helpful. I do a daily 10-minute meditation).
  • A – Affirmations (an example is ‘everything always works out for me).
  • V – Visioning (close your eyes and envision one of your goals or tasks being done).
  • E – Exercise (run or walk up and down the stairs for a minute or stretch for 1-10 minutes).
  • R – Reading (every little bit helps – I am reading 10 pages each in the morning and the night).
  • S – Scribing (writing or journaling is a great habit).

​

Elrod shares his personal story of overcoming adversity, including surviving a serious car accident and facing financial ruin, to illustrate how the Miracle Morning routine helped him turn his life around. He argues that anyone, regardless of their current circumstances, can benefit from waking up early and dedicating time to this routine.

​

The book provides practical advice on how to implement the Life S.A.V.E.R.S. into one's morning routine, including tips for waking up early, even for those who do not consider themselves morning people. Elrod emphasizes the importance of consistency and personalization, encouraging readers to adapt the practices to fit their own needs and goals.

​

Overall, "The Miracle Morning" is a motivational guide that promises to transform lives by showing how the first hour (or six minutes) of the day can be used to focus on self-improvement and set the tone for a successful and fulfilling day.
