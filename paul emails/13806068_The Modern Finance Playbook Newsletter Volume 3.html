A winning framework: The ‘What,’ ‘So What’ and ‘Now What’

​

This model is an extremely useful model developed by Terry Borton in the 1970s in the medical industry. It is a great framework that can applied to Finance. One of our clients (Deepak Bhandari, uses this framework as a philosophy for his team).

​

The ‘What’ – this is very basic. What happened? For example, Revenue was up 50%, driven by customer A or product B.

​

The ‘So What’ – this is the next step. What is the implication of ‘the what.’ In our example, we are behind our quarterly projection by x% as a result of this miss on customer A or Product B. The ‘So What’ is much more helpful to our stakeholders than just ‘the what.’

​

The ‘Now What’ – this is where Finance can make a massive difference and be true value-added business partners. This is a forward-looking approach. What action can we take as a result of ‘the what’ and ‘so what.’

​

In our example, we can bring scenarios to the table to say that as a result of the miss on customer A or product B, how can we still achieve our quarterly targets with promotions at other customers or other adjustments across P&L lines.

The FP&A Guy's Tech Tips

​

Today's tech tip is a reminder that technology is not a solution it is only an enabler. If you want to have a great FP&A department, you need to focus on three areas.

​

  • People - Make sure you have the right people in the right roles with the right skills.
  • Process - Make sure you are regularly reviewing processes for opportunities to streamline, optimize, and automate your processes. Create a culture focused on process optimization.
  • Technology - Thoughtfully implemented technology can serve as an enabler, allowing your team to accomplish more.

​

Once you have the right people and have created a mindset focused on continuously improving your processes, then you are ready to implement technology. Technology combined with the first two is the enabler that allows magic to happen for your Finance/FP&A department.

Ron's Recommendations

​

The Go-giver by Bob Burg and John David Mann

The Go-Giver was a game-changing book for me. It uniquely paints the picture of how ‘giving’ is a winning strategy using an interesting story. Many personal development gurus like Oprah or Wayne Dyer say the same thing in different ways. What you put out into the universe will come back to you.

​

The book is based on a story where Joe (the main character) is struggling with his life and comes across Pindar, who is a wealthy, connected, and humble person. Pindar took Joe under his wing and introduced him to a series of people who taught him the five laws for stratospheric success.

​

Finance individuals and teams can learn a lot from this philosophy. The Go-giver contains some fantastic secrets to help gain a seat at the table and have a rewarding and fulfilling career and life.

​

Here are the five laws and one specific thing Paul and I did as a result of this book's teachings:

​

1) The Law of Value – your true worth is determined by how much more you give than you take in payment (receive).

​

2) The Law of Compensation – your income is determined by how many people you serve and how well you serve them.

​

3) The Law of Influence – your influence is determined by how abundantly you place other people’s interests first.

​

4) The Law of Authenticity – the most valuable gift you have to offer is yourself.

​

5) The Law of Receptivity – the key to effective giving is to stay open to receiving.

​

A great little book that I frequently refer to in order for me to bring my best and authentic self every day!
