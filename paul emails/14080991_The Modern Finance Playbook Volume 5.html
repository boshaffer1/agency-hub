Stay out of Excel Hell

Avoid building a "Frankein-Model" that the person who replaces you will replace as soon as they start. These 12 tips will help you better design your Financial Models.

​

  • Plan and design your model upfront
  • Include an assumptions tab/section – ask the right questions
  • Structure the file (inputs, calculations, outputs)
  • Include Model Instructions
  • Include cover page, executive summary, and table of contents
  • Use schedules for calculations
  • Build a scenario section for your model
  • Use color coding to distinguish (inputs, links, calculations, etc.)
  • Never hardcode data into formulas
  • Keep formulas simple
  • Limit file sizes by using best practices
  • Include error checks throughout model

​

The most important for me when it comes to modeling is design and that is why I love the below quote by Ian Schnoor.

“I will tell you I’ve probably built over a hundred models in my career. I’ve seen thousands. The number one reason why models fail, and there’s not even a close second is poor design. Full stop it is poor design And so it always comes down to effective flow, data flow, and data design.“

​

- Ian Schnoor, Executive Director of Financial Modeling

Bottom Line Up Front (BLUF)

​

How often have you been in a meeting or presentation where you want to blurt out ‘please get to the point’? I have painfully experienced this many times.

​

The BLUF approach is a communication strategy that emphasizes clarity and efficiency by presenting the main point or key message at the beginning of a presentation, email, or report. This approach is particularly crucial in today's fast-paced business environment, where audiences and business leaders often have limited time and attention spans.

​

By leading with the essential information, the BLUF approach ensures that recipients quickly grasp the main idea, enabling more effective decision-making and streamlined communication.

​

Transitioning to a BLUF approach requires a shift in communication habits.

​

  • Firstly, start by clearly identifying the primary message or key takeaway you want to convey. This may involve distilling complex information into a concise statement that captures the essence of your message.

​

  • Secondly, organize your communication in a way that places the most critical information at the forefront, whether it's in an email, report, or presentation. Prioritize clarity and brevity to keep the audience engaged and focused on the main point.

​

  • Finally, consider the perspective of your audience. Tailor your communication to address their specific needs and concerns, ensuring that the BLUF approach resonates effectively.

​

In essence, adopting the BLUF approach is a commitment to respecting the time and priorities of your audience and is a great business partnership strategy.

​

By leading with the bottom line, you increase the likelihood that your message will be understood and acted upon swiftly, fostering more efficient and productive communication in both professional and personal contexts.

Ron's Recommendations

​

Success Principles by Jack Canfield

This fantastic book was a game-changer for me, and I frequently refer to it. Jack Canfield is a leadership and personal development guru who has authored several best-selling books. Success principles is a phenomenal and extremely comprehensive book containing sixty-seven sequential life principles.

​

Jack compares the sequence of principles to a combination lock where the order of applying these principles does matter.

​

The book starts with principle 1 which is taking 100% accountability for your current life and then getting crystal clear on your purpose and goals. It then provides a fantastic series of principles and practices with several interesting and compelling stories to help you illustrate the power of following these principles. The book covers self-transformation, building a high-performing team, creating successful relationships, money, as well as a section focused on success in the digital age.

​

Some of my favourite principles are:

​

2) Be clear why you are here (spending time digging into our own individual gifts and purpose).
4) Believe it’s possible (developing confidence and belief takes deliberate practice).
17) Ask, Ask, Ask (In one of Jack Canfield’s books he uses the term ‘askhole’ which means that we should keep asking for what we want).
24) Exceed expectations.
41) Take action.
59) Master the art of effective communication.
65) Develop empowering success habits.

​

During covid, I read a chapter every few days and journaled about how I can apply the learnings. I highly recommend this approach.
