Business Partners Corner

​

Being a trusted business partner is something that is important to Finance colleagues.

​

We believe it is about using our 360-degree view of the business and command of the numbers to be invited to the table and help make great business decisions.

​

Becoming a trusted business partner involves an investment.

​

Here are 5 steps to becoming a great business partner:

1) Invest time in learning the business & building relationships with your stakeholders. Be genuinely interested in their roles and needs and actively listen when you meet them. Takes steps to learn the business well beyond your Finance role. Examples would be proactively visiting manufacturing sites, attending sales meetings. Genuine interest usually leads to reciprocity.

​

2) Get quick wins. One of my favourite CFOs (Jamie Chiarotto, Skjodt-Barrett foods) gave me a great example. Early in one of his roles, he started doing quick 15-minute meetings with his sales partners to understand results rather than have them all fill out cumbersome templates. He solved a major pain point and earned trust and wedged himself into the room. What is a quick win for you that helps your stakeholders?

​

3) Use simple language and stay away from complex financial jargon. Using complex financial jargon is often a way to distance yourself from your business partners. Think of the person in the room with the least financial comprehension and cater to them.

​

4) Focus on the future. An extremely helpful framework* is:

​

  • ‘The what’ - explain what happened in the business.
  • ‘The so what’ - analyze and explain the implications.
  • ‘The now what’ – clearly recommending next steps to help improve future business results.

    The more you focus on b and c, the more you will become a better business partner.

​

5) Keep it short and sweet. Another fantastic framework that my colleague Paul learned is to use the BLUF acronym. This is not about misleading anyone (😉) but it stands for Bottom Line Up Front. Use the appendix for supplementary information and keep it concise.
​

Business partnership is possible if you invest the time and show that you are on the team!

​

*This model was created by Terry Borton as a group facilitation technique in the 1970s before it was popularised as a reflective tool for clinical healthcare practitioners in the 1980s.

Ron's Recommendations

​

Developing a consistent habit of learning in different ways can be a game changer for any Finance professional. Warren Buffet talks about the compounding effect of reading, which has been one of the main factors that have propelled him to success.

​

“I sit in my office and read all day” Warren Buffett

Here are three key game-changing habits I adopted from this classic.

​

  • Habit stacking - I had unsuccessfully tried to build a meditation practice into my schedule before reading this book. After reading this book I made a rule that if I didn’t do a 10-minute meditation first thing in the morning, no coffee for me. That instantaneously made me commit to the practice. The habit is now engrained!

​

  • Environment trumps willpower - James talks about working on your environment rather than relying on willpower. If you want to get healthy, remove junk food from your pantry or fridge. If you want to avoid social media, delete it from your home screen. I did this with Instagram and my endless scrolling went down dramatically.

​

  • Start small - The example he uses is to just spend a few minutes on what you are trying to do. For example, if you are trying to improve your Microsoft skills, learn one new formula a day or a week.

Excel Unique Function Detailed Description

Unique Function - Returns a unique list of values from a range/list.

Syntax: Unique (array, [by_col],[exatly_once])

Array - This is your data set, data array or range from which to return unique elements.

[by_col]- This is an optional argument that requires a value indicating how to compare the data.

TRUE - Will compare columns against each other and return the unique columns.

FALSE - Will compare rows against each other and return unique rows. By default if you do not enter this argument it assumes false.

[exactly_once] - This is an optional argument and will return only a unique data set for items that only appear once in the data.

TRUE - Returns a unique list from the rows or columns that only appear once in the data set.

FALSE - will return a unique list regardless of how many times something appears in the dataset. This is the default operation, and if you enter, nothing assumes false.

Note: [] = Optional input not required.

​
